# 1.26268 — 25 September 2026

Seven days after **1.26261**. This release is about getting work started without a detour and then seeing it through: you can attach a recipe to the message you are about to send, hand it the files it should work on, and let a single gezel — one of the named AI craftspeople in your crew — carry the job from the first step to the last. The first ready-made knowledge catalogs also arrive — ten subject shelves drawn from Wikipedia that your crew can search without going online.

If you read only one thing: the chat box now has a **Task** key. Pick a craftbook — one of gezel's saved recipes for a piece of work — and the message you are writing becomes its brief. Press **Start the task** and the work begins right away, without waiting for a model to decide what you meant.

## Start a craftbook from the message you are writing

Until now, starting a **craftbook** from chat meant asking your **meester** — the guildmaster gezel who coordinates your crew — and hoping the model reached for the right recipe. The only sure route was Tasks → New task, away from the conversation you were in.

On a fresh thread, the composer now has a **Task** key beside Send. It opens **Task for this message**, where you choose a craftbook and fill in anything it needs. The message you were typing is shown as the brief, and editing it there edits the chat box too. **Use in chat** attaches it: a strip appears above your message with the craftbook's artwork, its name and the settings you chose. Click the strip to change them, or the × to take the task off. Nothing runs until you send.

With a craftbook attached, Send becomes **Start the task**. The task is created on the spot with your message as its description, and the thread keeps a receipt naming the new task. A double click or a retried send reuses that task instead of starting a second one, and the attachment is saved with your draft, so it survives closing the app.

Gezel will also suggest one. Last release showed a small label naming where a request for a PowerPoint or PDF would go; that label is now a dashed **Suggested task** strip you can start with Send or dismiss with ×. Suggestions are no longer limited to document formats. Nearly every craftbook in the catalog lists a few phrases it answers to — "make an invoice", "board deck", "prep me for the meeting" — and chats with the meester or a project **voorman** (foreman) offer the matching book when your message alone is enough to start it. Dismiss a suggestion and the message goes as ordinary chat; the same suggestion will not come back for the same text.

Launch forms are shorter, too. A form now asks only what a person can answer: settings gezel fills in itself, such as the task's working folder, are no longer shown, and a recipe that needs a source file, a topic, or source material shows all three as ordinary fields and tells you to fill in one.

## Point a craftbook at your own files

Some recipes work *on* something: a folder of meeting notes to turn into minutes, a set of chapters to compile into an ebook, a pile of source documents to turn into an FAQ. Until now the only way to say which files was to type a path, and some of these craftbooks had nowhere to say it at all.

Those craftbooks now have a **Works on** section in their launch form, with two keys. **In this project** lets you choose a folder or file from the project's workspace, read where it is, with a live count of how many files and how much data you picked. **From your computer** lets you choose or drag in files and folders from anywhere on your machine. Gezel copies them into the task's own folder, so your originals are never touched, and lists anything it left out and why — too large, or not a type the craftbook reads. A recurring task can use only files in the project, because it reads them fresh on every run.

Every step of the task is told where the files are and how to open them, and no gezel can change the copy it was handed. Craftbooks that use this today include Meeting Minutes & Actions, Meeting Notes to Action Items, Standup / Team Update Summary, FAQ from Source Docs, Ebook Compile and Content Accuracy Review.

## Wikipedia on the shelf

A **knowledge catalog** is a packaged body of reference material your crew can search and quote, naming the source every time. Catalogs arrived a few releases ago (see [1.26234](1.26234.md)), but until now you had to build or find one yourself. **Settings → Knowledge** now offers the first ten ready to install, each a subject shelf drawn from the English Wikipedia as of April 2026:

- **Arts & Architecture** — painting, sculpture, movements, dance
- **Astronomy & Space** — the solar system, stars, galaxies, spaceflight
- **Food & Drink** — dishes, ingredients, drinks, cuisines
- **Games** — video games, board and tabletop games, card games
- **Language & Linguistics** — languages and their families, dialects, writing systems
- **Law** — legislation, treaties, courts, legal concepts
- **Mathematics** — theorems, structures, numbers, algorithms, statistics
- **Medicine & Health** — conditions, anatomy, medications, mental health
- **Religion & Philosophy** — religions, scripture, mythology, philosophy
- **Science** — chemistry, physics, earth and life sciences

Together they hold about 150,000 articles. Each shelf is a single download of between roughly 120 MB and 550 MB, so install the subjects you care about rather than all of them; the first one also fetches a small search model. Once installed, the search runs entirely on your device, so a gezel can look something up with no internet connection and no account. A **Knowledge** area appears in the sidebar where you can browse the shelf yourself, with a **Copy citation** button on every article, and when catalog excerpts are put in front of a reply, they are listed under it so you can see exactly what the model read. Each project decides which catalogs it sees in its settings. The articles are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, which is why the source travels with every quote.

## One gezel can carry a whole task

A task in gezel is a craftbook walked one step at a time, with a check at the end of each step. Until now each step went to a specialist for that trade — a researcher, a writer, a reviewer — each starting a fresh conversation and picking up from the notes the last one left. That is the right shape for small models on your own device, which do best in short, focused conversations. For large hosted models it mostly meant re-reading.

**Generalist mode** puts one gezel, the [Generalist](../gezel-roles/role-generalist.md), on the whole task in a single conversation. It sees the full outline, works the active step, passes that step's check and moves on, so what it learned in step two is still in front of it in step five. Nothing is skipped: every step and every check is the one a crew would walk, work that fans out into many small tasks still runs in parallel, a task you assigned to a particular gezel stays theirs, and a step that is yours to approve stays yours.

It lives in **Settings → Artificial Intelligence → Run in generalist mode**. **Automatic**, the default, turns it on for Claude, ChatGPT and Codex, and GitHub Copilot, and leaves it off for models running on this device. **On** and **Off** force it either way. The choice is made when a task is created, so changing it never disturbs work already under way. If you reach Claude or ChatGPT with your own API key, a new project the meester starts for you on Automatic now begins with one lead gezel rather than a full crew, as it already did for Copilot; choose **Off** if you prefer the crew. [Generalist mode](../conceptual/generalist-mode.md) has the details.

## The meester sees its own work through

More of what you ask the meester for — a deck, a report, an invoice run — now runs as a task in the **Default** project, the one gezel uses when you have not picked another. Several things there were built for a time when Default held no real work, and they got in the way:

- A step that finishes by saving its file now moves on when the file is saved. In Default it could not, and the task paused after doing the work correctly.
- One unanswered question in the meester's chat no longer freezes every task in Default, and an unrelated failed chat no longer pauses a healthy one.
- A step the meester owns that goes quiet now gets a nudge, and pauses to ask for help if it stays stuck, instead of waiting indefinitely.
- A craftbook that names no particular trade now goes to the meester instead of sitting "active" with nobody working on it.

You can also talk to the meester about that work. "Cancel the PowerPoint task" or "how is the deck going?" now reaches the task already under way instead of starting a second one, and the meester can pause, resume or cancel the runs it launched. It restarts a paused task only when you ask, never on its own initiative. A request for "some slides for our team" stays in the project you are in instead of turning into a new project, and asking twice for the same thing no longer launches two crews.

## Smaller things

- **On a Mac, on-device models that can see now see your images.** An MLX model with a vision component receives the pictures you attach, and the ones a gezel opens itself, as images rather than as a written description. Turns with images run a little slower because they skip the prompt cache. There is nothing to switch on.
- **Local vision models can look at images they open themselves.** On llama.cpp, a gezel whose model has its vision component loaded now sees an image it opens; without one, it is told plainly that no image was delivered instead of being told the call succeeded.
- **Narration can speak progress updates.** Settings → Audio → **Include progress updates** also reads out the short notes a gezel gives while it works, like "Now I'll draft the outline." It is on by default and matters only when narration is on, so if you already use narration you will start hearing these. Updates that fall behind are dropped rather than read minutes late.
- **Spoken replies use a new pronunciation dictionary.** Text-to-speech no longer depends on eSpeak NG; gezel carries its own American and British English dictionaries, and the British voices now speak British English. Some words will sound different, and a bug where the last sentence of a reply sometimes never played is gone.
- **A free source of reference photos.** The Web Access toolset gains a Wikimedia Commons image search that needs no account or key and returns the credit and license for each picture. It stays off wherever your security settings block outside services.
- **Small talk stays small.** A greeting like "Hey, how's it going?" no longer pulls unrelated files from your workspace into the reply, and a task's automatic context no longer draws on files from the project's other tasks.
- **Restoring a backup restores your settings.** The restore dialog never actually asked for the settings you ticked, so they were silently skipped; they are now restored. Ticking one item to replace no longer ticks an unrelated item of another kind.
- **Document search says when it cannot answer.** Instead of an empty list, the Documents area tells you when a search failed, when search is unavailable, or when results may be incomplete.
- **Local models start more reliably when other apps are using the graphics card.** On a PC with a separate graphics card, gezel now plans a model's memory against what is actually free rather than the card's total.
- **A long local turn is no longer cut short because the engine was too busy to answer a health check.** One such restart threw away twenty minutes of work.
- **The llama.cpp engine's saved-state cache is capped at 8 GB** and skips saving when your disk is nearly full; it used to grow without limit.
- **Tasks keep moving.** A step that fanned out into child tasks picks up as soon as the children finish instead of several minutes later; a step waiting for a busy engine gets its turn within a minute; a step's check no longer repeats an old "file not found" after the file has been written; and a task that cannot be started pauses and asks for help instead of sitting silent.
- **On-device models on a Mac stop when their tool call is done** instead of repeating closing markup until they hit their length limit, and Gemma 4 tool calls are garbled less often.
- **Claude CLI and Codex CLI can pass craftbooks that check the test run.** Those checks look for a record gezel's own script runners write, and both providers used to have those runners hidden.
- **The meester's Home chat opens on a fresh thread** when you start gezel instead of reopening the last one; unsent drafts are still there. A launch with first-run setup unfinished always lands on Home.
- **Scripts cut off by closing gezel are marked failed** at the next start, with a note that their actions were not replayed. With advanced features on, the Scripts view can also browse and run the built-in **Standard library**.
- **Keyboard focus returns to the button that opened a dialog** when the dialog closes, and a gezel's name is now a proper **Rename gezel** button.
- Catalog content moved from `@bendyline/gilde` 0.1.67 to 0.1.73.

## For developers

- **Craftbook inputs.** A `paramSchema` property with `"input": { "kind": "folder" }` (or a file kind) gets the **Works on** picker. `TaskManager.create` resolves it before interpolation, writes a manifest, stamps `Task.inputs`, and names the drawer and its tools in every step prompt; the adopted folder is write-denied to gezels.
- **Launch-form annotations.** `askUser: false` hides a param another surface fills and `askUser: true` forces one back; a param whose default is a runtime template (`{{task.dir}}`) is never shown. `fromMessage: true` names the param a chat message fills, and a book's `triggers` feed the suggestion strip.
- **`POST /api/sessions/:id/launch-task`** creates a craftbook task from a chat message with no model turn, sharing invocation-key dedupe with the MCP `invoke_craftbook` tool. A send carrying `turnIntent: 'off'` stands the turn-intent routing down for that turn.
- **New gate check `artifactReadEvidence`**, and a terminal step may complete when its deliverable is saved.
- **New MCP tools:** `manage_task` (pause, resume, cancel; resume only on a user-directed turn) in the meester's `task-oversight` kit, and `wikimedia_image_search`.
- **Generalist mode** is `config.generalistMode: 'auto' | 'on' | 'off'`; the older `executionDensity` key is migrated once, and each task is stamped with `executionMode` at creation.
- **App SDK hosting.** A saved grant is reused before hosting, `hostWhenRefused` hosts a private daemon even after the user declines, a hosted daemon always takes an ephemeral port, and `host.nativeBinDir` with the new `@bendyline/gezel-service/native-release` export lets an application ship its own engines. See [Building connected apps with gezel-app-sdk](../technical/building-connected-apps-with-gezel-app-sdk.md).
- **`gezel status` exits 1** when the service is not running or not healthy, so scripts can test it directly.

## Things to see and do after update

Open a fresh thread with your meester, press **Task** and pick a craftbook — or just describe what you want and watch for a suggested task above your message. Visit **Settings → Knowledge** and install a Wikipedia shelf or two for the subjects you work in. If you use a hosted model, look at **Settings → Artificial Intelligence → Run in generalist mode**; Automatic is a good place to stay.
