# Social mode

**Social mode** is one switch in **Settings → General**. With it on, your crew chats in character, you can watch each of them grow, and opening a chat shows you what is waiting. With it off, they work in a plain, even voice and none of this is on screen.

It starts on for phones and off for the desktop. Your choice wins on either one.

## Character

Every crew member is born with a **character**: four small choices that shape how they come across in chat.

- **Temperament**: warm, calm, lively, precise, wry, or patient.
- **Quirk**: curious, tidy, storyteller, planner, frugal, cheerleader, skeptic, wordsmith, rememberer, punctual, straight-shooter, or encourager.
- **Style**: plain, playful, or formal.
- **Sociability**: how much small talk they make, from Quiet to Talkative.

A character is picked from a crew member's id when they are created and then kept as it is, so they stay the same person. You can change any of it on their **Appearance** tab. Under the choices, you'll see the exact lines they read about themselves, so nothing a choice does is hidden.

Character is about *how* someone talks, not *what* they can do. Their role, toolkit, and the description on their **About** tab stay in charge of the work. A character never makes anyone skip a step or cut a corner. It decides whether they open with a joke or get straight to the point.

Some choices also have a practical effect. A quiet, calm, or frugal character keeps table talk short. In a game like checkers, an opponent set to the default *in-character* personality plays in their own character.

## Growth

Your crew gets better at helping you over time, and growth makes that visible. Each crew member earns experience from what they **learn**: preferences and decisions they write down, corrections they take on board, lessons they distill, and work they finish. Chatting more does not earn anything. Neither does sending lots of messages.

When one of them reaches a new level, they offer you a short menu of changes, each grounded in what they've actually learned:

- **a trait**: a working habit drawn from their notes (for example, "Show a worked example before stating the rule"),
- **a tuning change**: a little more deliberate, or a little more exploratory,
- **a cosmetic**: a new hat, apron, or accessory for their figure.

You choose one, or skip the level. Nothing changes until you say yes, and you can retire a trait later from the **Growth** tab.

Growth keeps counting with social mode off. You just won't see the Growth tab, the level badges, or the "I just reached level 3!" note in chat until you turn it back on.

Growth works the same on phones as on the desktop. On a phone, level-up suggestions are drafted quietly in the background with the on-device model, and that drafting pauses whenever the app does.

Your own progress is separate. Your Spanish level or a workout streak belongs to the project you're working in. It never counts toward a crew member's level.

## What's waiting

With social mode on, opening someone's chat shows **at most one** thing they have waiting for you:

- a question they asked that you haven't answered yet, or
- work they finished in the last few days, with a button to open it.

If there's nothing waiting, there's no card. Gezel doesn't invent a greeting to fill the space. Dismiss a card and it stays gone.
